## What failed / limitations observed

1. **A tall ring reads high — the one systematic error in the set.** The hoop is ≈ 3 mm tall; every one of its 13 photos reads +0.3 to +0.5 mm against the gauge, with no dependence on which surface it lies on. Three checks say this is not the algorithm:
   the found boundary drawn back onto the full-resolution photo sits on the visible inner edge; the marker scale was verified independently against the display's own pixel grid (0.1119 mm pitch, FFT on `H-paper-05`: 24.6–24.9 photo px/mm vs 24.67 from the marker); and a photo of the ring over the 17.5 mm gauge circle shows the line at the edge of the hole while the 17.9 mm line is covered. What the camera sees is the top of the inner rim, 2–3 mm closer to the lens than the marker on the sheet, and it projects larger by roughly height ÷ distance (2–3 % at 11–21 cm). The gauge reads the narrowest part of the hole, which is what a finger feels. The σ model already carries this as the parallax term (sized for a 2 mm band, so the hoop lands at ≈ 1 σ); the point estimate is **not** corrected because the ring's height is not observable from one top-down photo, and a fixed correction would have pushed the thin band's error the other way. Stated on the Limits page. The thin band, ≈ 1 mm tall, shows no such bias.
2. **Bare glass gives a false inner edge.** With the ring directly on the display, a dark reflection band inside the rim pulled the least-squares circle 0.5 mm small on some rays. Two changes: the reported diameter is now the largest circle inscribed in the 64-point rim polygon (what a gauge passes) rather than the least-squares circle, and the photos were repeated with a square of white paper under the ring. Both surfaces are in the set; they agree within 0.1 mm for each ring.
3. **A thin hammered band was lost once** (`B-paper-04`, first run: `NO_RING`). The 7 px morphological opening that detaches highlights from a solid band cut the 0.9 mm band itself, so the hole leaked into the paper. Fixed with a second pass at 3 px when the first finds nothing; the photo now measures 16.90 mm.
4. **Hand-held overhead shots are tilted 14–17°** (marker side ratio 0.958–0.97). The original gate (0.97) rejected three ordinary photos as `TILT`. Relaxed to 0.95 (≈ 18°); the residual tilt now feeds σ through the marker keystone as well as the hole's ellipticity. A deliberate 40° shot still rejects (cos 40° = 0.77).
5. **Heavy motion blur falls through to `NO_MARKER`, not `BLUR`** — the marker gate runs first and cannot find the marker at all (`U-blur-heavy`). Recorded as expected `NO_MARKER` rather than pretending the blur gate fired. The mild case, where the marker is still found and the blur gate is the one that speaks, is `U-blur` (score 29 against a threshold of 60).
6. Tilt below ≈ 5° is not resolvable from a 20 mm marker or a 17 mm hole; carried as an assumed ± 5° term in σ.
7. Two objects only. Both rings I own; the range 12–26 mm is claimed on the synthetic tests, verified on real photos at 16.8 and 17.5 mm.
8. All five reject paths that a user can trigger are in the set and fire for the right reason: 39° tilt (`U-tilt40`, marker side ratio 0.78), motion blur with the marker still readable (`U-blur`), two rings in the zone (`U-two-rings`), marker out of frame (`U-no-marker`), marker destroyed by blur (`U-blur-heavy`).

### What changed after the first photos (gate calibration, 21–22.09)

| constant | before | after | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| reported diameter | least-squares rim circle | largest inscribed circle; rim circle kept for the overlay | glass reflection (2); an open hoop with a hinge is not a circle |
| `EDGE_MAX_RESIDUAL_PX` | 0.6 | 2.5 | a hammered band lying flat scatters 1.1–2.2 px; residual also feeds σ |
| `EDGE_MIN_INLIERS` | 48 | 44 | an open hoop keeps 46–48 of 64 rays |
| `MIN_AXES_RATIO` | 0.97 | 0.94 | the faceted band reads 0.95–0.99 while flat; tilt is caught by the marker |
| `MIN_SIDE_RATIO` | 0.97 | 0.95 | (4) |
| `TYPICAL_SIGMA_MM` | 0.3 (placeholder) | 0.55 | ≥ max observed error, as the rule in the code says |
| hole finder | 7 px opening | 7 px, then 3 px | (3) |

## AI tools and models

- Claude Code with Claude Opus 5 — planning, code, tests, evaluation, this document. Reused components: OpenCV.js (`@techstark/opencv-js`: ArUco detector, contours, ellipse fit, distance transform), pdf-lib, Vite/React/Tailwind, Playwright. Own code: sheet geometry, gates, radial refinement + consensus circle fit, inscribed circle, uncertainty model, sizing mapping, UI, evaluation runner.
- Where the AI's output was checked and corrected:
  - The sizing constants it first wrote (UK A = 37.8 mm) failed the anchor test against the published table; corrected to the cited definition (C = 40 mm, 1.25 mm per size). Five published anchor rows are pinned in `tests/sizing.test.ts`.
  - The first inscribed-circle implementation used OpenCV's 5×5 distance-transform approximation and read 0.28 mm low on the synthetic test ring; switched to the precise transform and sub-sampled the polygon, back to ≤ 0.1 mm.
  - It proposed that the three `TILT` rejects were the deliberate 40° shots. The marker side ratio said 0.958–0.97 = 14–17°; they were ordinary hand-held photos, which is how the gate got recalibrated instead of the photos being relabelled.
  - It first attributed the hoop's +0.4 mm to the algorithm and tried an edge-search variant that shifted every result 1.5 px; reverted, and the three checks in "What failed" (1) were done instead of tuning until the number matched.
  - A full-resolution corner refinement it added measured no change on any photo (quad identical); kept as harmless, noted here so it is not mistaken for a contributor.

## What I would improve next

1. **Ring height.** Either one tap ("thin band / thick band") or a second photo at a known angle; then the parallax term becomes a correction and the hoop's +0.4 mm disappears. This is the whole of the systematic error observed.
2. Distance coaching on the capture screen ("move back to ≈ 25 cm") — halves the same bias for free and costs 0.1 mm of resolution.
3. A VLM "retake coach" (Gemini Flash / Claude Haiku, ~$0.002/image) that explains rejects in plain language from the photo — only on reject, never on the measurement path.
4. More objects: a plain round band and a comfort-fit ring, to separate "tall" from "hinged" in the error table.
